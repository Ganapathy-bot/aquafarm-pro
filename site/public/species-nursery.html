Know Your Fish (Major Carps)

3 Niches

Catla

Catla catla (Katol)
Surface Feeder

Broad head & upturned mouth. Feeds mainly on zooplankton, surface insects, and floating natural organisms.

Rohu

Labeo rohita (Rui)
Column Feeder

Streamlined body, sub-terminal fringed lips. Feeds on suspended algae, microphytes, phytoplankton, and submerged detritus.

Mrigal

Cirrhinus mrigala (Naini)
Bottom Feeder

Linear body, downward-pointing mouth. Consumes benthic organisms, decaying organic matter, keeping bottom sediments balanced.

3-Stage Growth Progression

1
Spawn→Fry Nursery
2
Fry→Fingerling Rearing
3
Grow-out Grow-out

Nursery stage: spawn to fry. Rearing stage: fry to fingerling. Grow-out stage: fingerling to marketable fish. Advanced fingerlings around 8–12 cm are preferable for grow-out stocking.

Nursery Management Protocol

4 Critical Steps
1

Seed Procurement & Acclimatization

General

Purchase healthy fish seed from a reliable hatchery. Choose active, uniform seed. Reject seed that is injured or swimming abnormally.

2

Nursery Pond Preparation

Pre-Stocking

Remove unwanted fish and predators. Remove excessive aquatic weeds. Check pond bunds and water inlets. Apply lime and fertilizers based on soil and water conditions. Encourage natural food production.

3

Rearing & Water Parameter Monitoring

Daily Checks

Maintain suitable water quality. Provide appropriate supplementary feed. Monitor growth, mortality, and stocking density. Rice bran and groundnut oil cake are discussed on the pond management screen. Do not substitute rice husk for rice bran.

4

Fingerling Grading & Transfer

To Grow-out

Advanced fingerlings around 8–12 cm are preferable for grow-out stocking. Grade, inspect, and acclimatize the fish before transfer.

How to read the numbers

Around 8–12 cm is a general preferred size for stocking a grow-out pond. Measure the fish you stock. Do not treat that size as a result from one farm.
